ECU Control Functions

  1. Startup sequencing — igniter power-up, fuel priming, detonation initiation
  2. Fuel metering — closed-loop fuel flow control via servo valve position
  3. Detonation monitoring — wave speed, count, and stability from high-speed pressure data
  4. MHD power regulation — generator load matching, accelerator current control
  5. Thermal protection — monitor all TCs, trip if any wall exceeds limit
  6. Cooling control — pump speed modulation for fuel, water, and LN₂ circuits
  7. Magnet management — ramp-up/down sequencing for SC magnet coils
  8. Thrust modulation — throttle command → fuel flow + MHD power split
  9. Emergency shutdown — <50 ms full shutdown on any critical fault
  10. Health monitoring — real-time BITE, data logging at >1 MHz aggregate

Safety & Protection Electronics

Emergency Shutdown Triggers

  • Wall temperature > 1,400 K (any TC)
  • Chamber pressure > 30 bar
  • Detonation wave loss > 500 ms
  • Fuel pressure drop > 20% from setpoint
  • Coolant flow < 50% of nominal
  • LN₂ level < 10%
  • MHD arc fault detected
  • Vibration > 50 g RMS
  • Manual E-STOP (hardwired, any of 3 stations)

Shutdown Sequence (<50 ms)

  1. Fire pyro fuel cutoff valves (both)
  2. De-energize MHD accelerator
  3. Open all coolant valves to 100%
  4. De-energize plasma igniter
  5. Ramp magnets to zero (60 s controlled)
  6. Maintain coolant flow for 5 min post-shutdown
  7. Log all sensor data through shutdown

Critical Thermal Risks & Mitigations

Cryogenic ↔ Hot Section Interface
The MHD section has 77 K magnets separated by only 200 mm from 3,000 K gas. The cryostat vacuum gap and MLI insulation must maintain <50 W heat leak. Any vacuum breach causes immediate magnet quench (superconductor loses superconductivity), resulting in rapid boil-off and potential structural damage from thermal shock. Mitigation: Redundant vacuum monitoring, automatic LN&sub2; dump valve, quench detection on every coil.
Combustion Cooling Channel Blockage
If any of the 48 fuel cooling channels become blocked (by coke, debris, or manufacturing defect), local wall temperature will exceed 1,800 K within seconds, causing C/SiC liner failure and burn-through. Mitigation: 10 μm fuel filter, thermal stability monitoring per channel (thermocouples), oversized channels (4×3 mm), and CT-scan inspection of all printed cooling passages before assembly.
Thermal Expansion Mismatch
C/SiC liners (CTE: ~3×10&supmin;&sup6;/K) expand much less than Inconel 718 shells (CTE: ~13×10&supmin;&sup6;/K). At operating temperature, the outer shell grows ~10 mm more in diameter than the liner. Mitigation: Compliant mounting (C-seals, spring clips, ceramic rope gaskets). Never bond rigidly. Design 3–5 mm radial clearance at room temperature.
Transient Thermal Shock (Start-up / Shutdown)
Rapid heat-up from 288 K to 3,200 K generates severe thermal gradients. C/SiC handles this well (low CTE, high thermal shock resistance), but Inconel and TBC coatings are vulnerable to spallation. Mitigation: Controlled ramp rate: 100 K/s max through combustion zone. RF plasma at low power before fuel introduction to pre-heat gradually.

Critical Material Notes

C/SiC at 4,000 K: RDE detonation temps exceed C/SiC service limit (1,800 K). TBC + EBC + regenerative cooling must keep wall temps below 1,800 K. If insufficient, UHTC liners (HfC, ZrB&sub2;) or transpiration cooling may be needed.

Print Procedure: IN718 Shells

RDE annulus and nozzle outer shells
  1. Export STL with 0.05 mm chord tolerance, add 0.5 mm stock on mating surfaces
  2. Orient part vertically (axis vertical), add support anchors at overhangs
  3. Load IN718 powder (15–53 μm) in argon atmosphere (<100 ppm O&sub2;)

  5. Wire EDM from build plate
  6. HIP: 1,165°C / 100 MPa / 4 hours in argon
  7. Solution treat: 980°C / 1 hr, air cool
  8. Double age: 720°C/8hr → furnace cool to 620°C/8hr → air cool
  9. CNC machine mating faces to ±0.05 mm
  10. CT scan + FPI + CMM dimensional inspection

Print Procedure: RDE Injector Face Plate

Monolithic IN625 disc with 24 integrated impinging-jet injectors
  1. Design internal fuel manifold with teardrop cross-section (self-supporting)
  2. 24 impinging-jet doublet passages aimed at annular gap
  3. Print flat (face plane horizontal) on EOS M290 or M400-4
  4. IN625 powder, 40 μm layers, 300 W, 800 mm/s
  5. Stress relief: 870°C / 1 hour

  8. Pressure test manifold: 70 bar hydrostatic (2× operating)

Testing & Validation

Test Sequence

  1. Cold Flow Test — Ambient air through engine. Measure pressure drops, verify mass flow capacity (100 kg/s target), check for leaks.
  2. Detonation Initiation Test — Reduced airflow (10–20%), energize plasma igniter, introduce fuel at low flow. Verify detonation initiation and wave stability via high-speed pressure transducers.
  3. RDE Characterization — Map detonation wave speed, count, pressure gain vs fuel flow. Increase airflow to 50% design point.
  4. MHD Generator Test — Cool magnets, verify field, inject alkali seed, measure gas conductivity and power extraction.
  5. MHD Accelerator Test — Power accelerator from generator output, measure thrust increment. Verify self-sustaining cycle.
  6. Full Engine Test — Ramp 25% → 50% → 75% → 100%. Measure thrust on calibrated stand. Record all parameters.
  7. Endurance Run — Sustained operation at rated thrust. Monitor for thermal creep, vibration, seal degradation, detonation stability.

Critical Engineering Challenges

1. RDE Detonation Stability
Maintaining continuous rotating detonation in an annular channel at 80–100 kg/s airflow is the primary challenge. Wave extinction, mode switching, and uneven fuel-air mixing can cause instability. This is an active area of research at AFRL, GE, and others.
2. Extreme Thermal Environment
Detonation temperatures of 3,500–4,000 K exceed C/SiC service limits (1,800 K). TBC + EBC + regenerative cooling must maintain wall temps below limits. Detonation wave thermal cycling at 1,500–2,000 Hz creates severe fatigue conditions.
3. MHD Generator–Accelerator Balance
The generator must extract enough power (300–600 kW) to drive the accelerator while maintaining positive net thrust. Getting this energy balance right requires precise control of electrode loading and magnetic field strength in both sections.
4. Gas Conductivity & Alkali Seeding
Higher RDE temps (3,500–4,000 K) improve natural ionization, but alkali seeding (K or Cs, 0.5–1%) is still needed for >10 S/m conductivity. Seed recovery and downstream contamination must be addressed.
